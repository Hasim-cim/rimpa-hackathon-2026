# Privacy Act: automated decision-making disclosure (APP 1.7–1.9), 19 Oct

Source asked about: a Lexology article (`g=617850f4-…`). ⚠️ Lexology returned 403, so it was **not read**.
The facts below come from the pages listed against each one.

## Verified (pages opened)

- **Commencement: 10 December 2026.** "On 10 December 2026, APP entities must include information in their
  privacy policy" where they use a computer program in certain decisions
  ([OAIC, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/new-resources-on-transparency-for-use-of-ai-and-automated-decision-making)).
- **Source Act:** Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 (No. 128, 2024), Schedule 1 Part 15
  "Automated decisions and privacy policies" ([legislation.gov.au](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00128/asmade/text),
  table of contents only; the clause text was not retrieved).
- **Trigger (APP 1.7), all three must hold:** a computer program is arranged to make a decision, or to do a
  thing substantially and directly related to making it; the decision could reasonably be expected to
  significantly affect an individual's rights or interests; and the individual's personal information is
  used in operating the program (OAIC page above).
- **Disclose (APP 1.8):** the kinds of personal information used; the kinds of decisions made *solely* by
  the program; and the kinds of decisions where a substantially related thing is done by the program.
- **OAIC resources (30 Sep 2026):** an APP 1.7–1.9 fact sheet, a supplementary fact sheet for government
  agencies, a flowchart, and updated APP 1 Guidelines. Informed by 90 written submissions.
- **Per [Allens, Oct 2026](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2026/10/mandatory-automated-decision-making-disclosure-requirements-are-coming-key-takeaways-from-the-oaic-guidance/)**
  (a law firm's reading of the guidance, not the guidance itself):
  - "Computer program" is read broadly: rule-based tools, AI/ML, everyday software including
    spreadsheets, generative AI and chatbots.
  - Human review does **not** take a tool out of scope if its output is "a key factor with a direct
    connection to the human's decision".
  - Likely significant domains: health, housing, finance, employment, utilities, plus a vulnerability test.
  - The obligation falls on the entity that "arranged for" the program (the user), not the vendor.
  - Categories are acceptable; no technical detail is required; sensitive data should be called out.
  - It is **transparency only**: no right to contest, no right to an explanation, no direct notification.
  - Recommended first step: an ADM audit that maps every program, including third-party tools, against
    the three tests.

## Snippets only (⚠️, check before a slide)

- ⚠️ The OAIC can issue infringement notices where a privacy policy lacks the content APP 1.4 requires
  (in force from 11 Dec 2024). The amounts reported differ between firms ($19,800 / $66,000 tiers, 200
  penalty units). Don't put a dollar figure on stage.
- ⚠️ The OAIC ran a privacy-policy compliance sweep in 2026.
- ⚠️ One source says the rules cover pre-existing ADM systems too. This is unconfirmed.

## Why it matters to us 💭

To comply, an organisation must know where programs feed significant decisions and what personal
information they use. That is a register of records, and most organisations don't have one. Record Check's
passport already captures the model, sources and human reviewer for each AI-assisted output, which is the
evidence such a register needs. See the framework proposal in the chat of 19 Oct; it is not adopted yet.
