# Knee surgery "once standard, now not recommended": can we use it on stage?

Markers: a link = I opened and read that page (PubMed/PMC pages were read through NCBI's E-utilities text API, same record; PDFs downloaded and read as text). ⚠️ = search snippet only. 💭 = my inference.

## Summary (5 lines)
1. Yes, usable, but only for **degenerative knee disease / uncomplicated knee osteoarthritis** and only as "guidance changed", never "doctors stopped doing it" or "it doesn't work for any knee".
2. The strongest safe anchor is the BMJ 2017 Rapid Recommendation ("strong recommendation against... nearly all patients with degenerative knee disease") plus the Australian Knee Society (for the AOA) 2019 statement; both keep exceptions (locked knee, other pathology).
3. "Standard practice" is defensible only as "very common" (BMJ: most common orthopaedic procedure in countries with data; >2 million/yr globally). Do not say "standard of care" without softening.
4. Do NOT say Medicare banned it: the 2021 MBS change is an explanatory note ("it is expected that arthroscopy should only be performed in patients with surgeon-confirmed obstructive symptoms") plus item descriptors, not a prohibition.
5. The ACSQHC Clinical Care Standard page was unreachable (timeouts); its wording is snippet-only (⚠️). Check it before quoting. We do not know which procedure the speaker meant (💭 arthroscopy is the best fit, not confirmed).

## Q1. Which procedure, and what do the trials and guidance actually say?

**Identification.** 💭 The prime candidate (knee arthroscopy for degenerative disease) is the best-documented "once common, now advised against" knee operation. We have no transcript saying which one the speaker meant. Ask the speaker/organiser if possible, or phrase the line so it does not name the practitioner's claim.

**Moseley 2002 (sham surgery, osteoarthritis).** [PubMed 12110735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12110735/): Moseley JB et al., N Engl J Med 2002 Jul 11;347(2):81-8, "A controlled trial of arthroscopic surgery for osteoarthritis of the knee". 180 patients randomised to arthroscopic debridement, lavage or placebo surgery. Conclusion, verbatim: "the outcomes after arthroscopic lavage or arthroscopic débridement were no better than those after a placebo procedure." Note: it tested lavage/debridement in OA, a single-centre US Veterans Affairs study.

**Sihvonen 2013 (FIDELITY).** [PubMed 24369076](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24369076/): N Engl J Med 2013 Dec 26;369(26):2515-24, "Arthroscopic partial meniscectomy versus sham surgery for a degenerative meniscal tear". 146 patients aged 35-65, degenerative medial meniscus tear, **no knee osteoarthritis**. Result: no significant between-group differences in Lysholm, WOMET or pain after exercise at 12 months. (Abstract was cut off before the conclusion line in my fetch; the results are as quoted.) Note the population differs from Moseley (no OA here); do not merge the two into "OA sham trial".

**BMJ Rapid Recommendation 2017.** [BMJ 2017;357:j1982, PMC5426368 full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5426368/) (publication date from [PubMed 28490431](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28490431/): 10 May 2017). Siemieniuk RAC, Harris IA (UNSW), ... Buchbinder R (Monash), et al. Verbatim:
- "We make a strong recommendation against the use of arthroscopy in nearly all patients with degenerative knee disease, based on linked systematic reviews; further research is unlikely to alter this recommendation"
- "This recommendation applies to patients with or without imaging evidence of osteoarthritis, mechanical symptoms, or sudden symptom onset"
- Definition: "degenerative knee disease" includes knee pain, particularly >35 years, with or without imaging OA, meniscus tears, locking/clicking except "persistent objective locked knee", acute or subacute onset; it excludes "recent debut of their symptoms after a major knee trauma with acute onset of joint swelling (such as haemarthrosis)".
- Exception noted by the panel: the evidence applies to any severity of mechanical symptoms "with the only possible exception being those who are objectively unable to fully extend their knee (that is, a true locked knee)". They "did not consider young patients with sports related injuries or patients with major trauma in any age."
- Benefit size: "in <15% of participants, arthroscopic surgery resulted in a small or very small improvement in pain or function at three months after surgery—this benefit was not sustained at one year."
- The recommendation is a patient-values judgement: "not applicable to patients who do not share these values".
- Trigger: a BMJ RCT of June 2016 (arthroscopy no better than exercise therapy). Table 2 of the article: "There are currently no updates to the article" (as at the PMC copy; I do not know the copy's date).

**Australian Knee Society (for the Australian Orthopaedic Association), updated April 2019.** [AKS position statement PDF](https://www.kneesociety.org.au/resources/Position-Statement-from-the-Australian-Knee-Society-on-Arthroscopic-Surgery-of-the-Knee-Final-2019.pdf), verbatim: "Arthroscopic debridement, and / or lavage, has been shown to have no beneficial effect on the natural history of osteoarthritis, nor is it indicated as a primary treatment in the management of osteoarthritis. However, this does not preclude the judicious use of arthroscopic surgery, when indicated, to manage symptomatic coexisting pathology, in the presence of osteoarthritis or degeneration. Partial medial meniscectomy is not indicated as an initial treatment for atraumatic tears of degenerative menisci, excluding bucket handle tears and surgeon assessed locked or locking knees." It lists scenarios where arthroscopy may still be appropriate (septic arthritis, symptomatic loose bodies, locked/locking knees, repairable tears, etc.). This is the best "doctors" source: the surgeons' own society. The MBS factsheet below cites an October 2016 version of this statement; I did not open that version.

**MBS (Medicare) changes, 1 July 2021.**
- [MBS Online factsheet "Changes to MBS Items for Orthopaedic Knee Surgery", last updated 1 July 2021](https://www.mbsonline.gov.au/internet/mbsonline/publishing.nsf/Content/F3E1DA9A21343EA6CA258646007893F7/$File/Factsheet-Knee%20.pdf): "The existing knee arthroscopy items have been replaced with 9 new items, separated into three tiers"; old items 49557-49563 replaced. Verbatim explanatory-note summary: "For patients with uncomplicated osteoarthritis it is expected that arthroscopy should only be performed in patients with surgeon-confirmed obstructive symptoms. Patient selection for knee arthroscopy in the presence of osteoarthritis should conform to the October 2016 Position Statement from the Australian Knee Society on Arthroscopic Surgery of the Knee."
- [Item map PDF](https://www.mbsonline.gov.au/internet/mbsonline/publishing.nsf/Content/F3E1DA9A21343EA6CA258646007893F7/$File/Arthroscopic-Knee-Surgery-Item-Map.pdf): the old debridement item 49558 ("1 or more of: debridement, osteoplasty or chrondroplasty") is among the deleted/replaced items; new item 49572 "Partial meniscectomy of knee, by arthroscopic means, for atraumatic meniscus tear, other than a service to which another item of this Schedule applies if the service described in the other item is for the purpose of treating osteoarthritis". 49580 covers meniscectomy for traumatic tear.
- What this is NOT: a statement that Medicare "no longer funds" knee arthroscopy, or that it is banned. It is a restructure plus a "should only be performed" expectation for uncomplicated OA. Debridement as such: I did not find a statement that debridement-for-OA has no Medicare item; I only saw the old items replaced. Say nothing more than the quote.
- Taskforce reports: news report on "no longer funded for uncomplicated osteoarthritis... locked or locking knee" is ⚠️ (The Limbic, snippet only; page returned 403) and is stronger than the factsheet wording. Do not use it. The Taskforce's own report text was not opened (health.gov.au docx surfaced in search, not opened). Which Taskforce report year: not found.

## Q2. Was it "standard practice"?
- Globally: [BMJ 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5426368/): "Arthroscopic knee surgery for degenerative knee disease is the most common orthopaedic procedure in countries with available data and on a global scale is performed more than two million times each year", costing "more than $3bn per year in the US alone". They also say financial incentives and prevalence of tears "may explain why arthroscopic knee surgery continues to be so common despite recommendations against its use" (so it was common, and in 2017 still common).
- Australia: [Lee, Awal, Vertullo, MJA 2020;212(1)](https://cdn.craft.cloud/019efd17-dcc2-707f-8e07-011d00d1146a/assets/issues/212_01_mja250436.pdf) (research letter, 13 Jan 2020; AIHW data, patients aged 50+, non-reconstructive arthroscopy): 512,214 operations in the 10 years July 2008 to June 2018; incidence rose from 756.2 per 100,000 (2008-09) to a peak 794.7 (2011-12; the 5% rise "statistically non-significant"), then fell to 483.5 (2017-18) (IRR 0.61; 95% CI 0.54-0.68). Arthroscopic meniscectomy rates fell 42% (588.0 to 340.1 per 100,000); meniscal repairs rose 258%. Authors: "We did not examine the drivers of the change"; they note the decline began shortly after the 2013 trials "and of ensuing medical society position statements", and that "the discrepancy between research findings and practice can take a decade to resolve." Says an earlier study found rates unchanged 2000-2008 (so it stayed common for a decade after Moseley 2002).
- 💭 Fair wording: "very common" or "routinely performed", not "standard of care". Drop "was standard, now banned". The Australian decline (about 39% in rate, 2011-12 to 2017-18, over 50s, all non-reconstructive arthroscopy) is from AIHW data and does not prove the guidance caused it.
- Not found: Australian pre-2008 volumes beyond the above; Moseley-era US volumes.

## Q3. "Medical reversal"
- Prasad V, Vandross A, Toomey C, ... Cifu A. "A decade of reversal: an analysis of 146 contradicted medical practices." [Mayo Clin Proc 2013 Aug;88(8):790-8, PubMed 23871230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23871230/). Reviewed 2044 original articles published 2001-2010 in one high-impact journal (abstract does not name the journal), 1344 about a medical practice; 363 tested an established practice; "146 (40.2%) reversed that practice, whereas 138 (38.0%) reaffirmed it". Definition: "reversal, when an existing practice is found to be no better than a lesser therapy". Conclusion: "The reversal of established medical practice is common and occurs across all classes of medical practice." Careful: 146 is of 363 established-practice articles (40.2%), not of all practices.
- Herrera-Perez D, Haslam A, Crain T, Gill J, ... Cifu AS, Prasad V. "A comprehensive review of randomized clinical trials in three medical journals reveals 396 medical reversals." [eLife 2019 Jun 11;8:e45183, PubMed 31182188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31182188/) and [full text via PMC6559784](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6559784/): "an analysis of more than 3000 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) published in three leading medical journals (JAMA, the Lancet, and NEJM), we have identified 396 medical reversals. Most of the studies (92%) were conducted on populations in high-income counties, cardiovascular disease was the most common medical category (20%), and medication was the most common type of intervention (33%)." Full text: JAMA and Lancet 2003-2017, NEJM 2011-2017; 7036 original articles screened, 3017 RCTs about a medical practice.
- Not found: whether either list includes knee arthroscopy (I did not search the lists). Do not claim Moseley is one of the 146/396 without checking.
- Caveat 💭: "reversal" means a trial found an established practice no better than a lesser therapy; it is the authors' construct, from trials in top journals.

## Lines we must NOT say
- "Knee surgery is no longer recommended" (unqualified) or "doctors no longer do it": arthroscopy is still indicated for locked knees, loose bodies, repairable tears, sepsis (AKS 2019, BMJ exceptions); only degenerative disease/uncomplicated OA is covered.
- "It was standard of care/standard practice" without softening: sources say "most common" and "very common", not "standard of care".
- "Medicare banned/no longer funds knee arthroscopy": the 2021 change is item restructuring plus an expectation for uncomplicated OA (factsheet); the "no longer funded" phrasing is a news paraphrase (⚠️).
- "Proved it doesn't work": the trials show no benefit over sham/exercise for specific populations; BMJ itself notes <15% had a small, short-lived benefit and the recommendation depends on patient values.
- "Moseley 2002 showed degenerative meniscal tears..." or "FIDELITY showed OA...": Moseley = OA lavage/debridement; FIDELITY = degenerative meniscal tear, no OA.
- "Guidance changed in 2002/2013": the recommendations came later (AKS 2016/2019, BMJ 2017, ACSQHC 2017/2024, MBS 2021). The trials were 2002 and 2013.
- Quoting the ACSQHC standard's wording as verbatim, or a quality-statement number, before opening it.
- "146 reversals = 146 of all practices" (it is 146 of 363 established-practice articles, 40.2%) or "396 reversals" attributed to one journal (three journals).
- "Australian arthroscopy rates fell because of the guidance" (MJA authors did not examine drivers).
- "The speaker's example was knee arthroscopy" (unconfirmed).
