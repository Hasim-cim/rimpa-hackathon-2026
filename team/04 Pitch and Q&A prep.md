# 🎤 Pitch and Q&A prep

## The 5-minute skeleton

| Time | Beat | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | **Hook** | One real case, told as a story | E.g. the AI scribe letter, or Deloitte's fake references |
| 0:30–1:15 | **The problem, in IM terms** | Name which of the four characteristics AI breaks | Authenticity, reliability, integrity, usability. The judges' own language |
| 1:15–2:00 | **Who and the gap** | The information manager, and what guidance says vs. what happens at the keyboard | "Guidance says *what* to capture. Nothing says *how*." |
| 2:00–3:30 | **Demo** | One path, one "aha" moment | Have the backup video ready |
| 3:30–4:15 | **Why it holds up** | The standards and guidance it implements, plus the long-term answer | ISO 23081, NAA's risk-based position, NSW AI metadata rule |
| 4:15–4:45 | **Value and scale** | Money or risk avoided; who pays; how it rolls out | Past winners all had a money line |
| 4:45–5:00 | **Close** | One memorable sentence | |

Then the team introduction, either at the start or the close. All three of us speak.

## Lines worth having ready

- "AI output used in business **is a record**. The Archives Act doesn't care who wrote it."
- "Content Credentials prove **who made** something, not **that it's true**."
- "The Robodebt Royal Commission: *'There simply were not records kept of who decided what, when.'*"
- "The NSW Supreme Court already requires that citation checks are **not done solely by AI**. We're giving every organisation that discipline."

## Things NOT to say

- "AI caused Robodebt." It was rules-based automation.
- "99-year retention." The Royal Commission recommended 45 years.
- "We fact-check the truth." Say we surface evidence for a human to judge.
- Any number tagged ⚠️ in the Background brief, until someone has opened the source.
